# Candidates

Three original lines drafted for the "Fortune Cookie Wisdom for Developers"
bounty, judged against the house brief: a tired senior engineer picks the
line that makes it smile and nod, weighing (1) it is one original line of
fortune-cookie wisdom for a developer, (2) it reads like a real fortune
cookie — one short balanced sentence that lands in a single breath,
(3) it says something true about a developer's day, (4) it turns that
truth in a way the reader has not heard before, and (5) it leaves a quiet
twist or double meaning that rewards a second read.

## 1. Picked (102 characters)

> Your legacy code is not a mess; it is a letter from a developer who loved this product before you did.

Why it wins: the classic fortune-cookie pivot ("not a mess; it is a
letter") reframes the daily pain of inherited code as a message of care
from a predecessor — a truth every developer who has opened an old
codebase knows, turned in a way no familiar saying does. The second read
lands the double meaning: you are now part of that chain of caretakers,
and the next letter will be yours.

## 2. Runner-up (87 characters)

> A quiet on-call night is the universe's way of saying your tests finally did their job.

Strong and short, but "the universe's way of saying" leans on generic
fortune phrasing, and a quiet on-call night is situational — not every
developer lives it. Its twist (silence as success, not luck) is good, but
thinner than the letter's.

## 3. Third (95 characters)

> The code review that stings today is the one you will quote fondly when you mentor someone new.

True about a developer's day and warm, but it reads more as encouragement
than as a fortune, and its turn — pain now, wisdom later — is a familiar
arc the judge has heard before.
